# Playing with Tess

Tess is not a screen that shows games. Her body is the toy. Every dot of her is
a corner of a tesseract or a point on one of its edges, and everything below
moves those corners. The dots between them follow on springs, so she wobbles,
stretches, rings and swings back like something physical. There is no menu,
score, button or instruction. You find everything by touching her the way you
already would.

## Things she does when you play

**Pluck.** Tap her. The corner nearest your finger rings, and the ring travels
through her edges one step at a time, to the 4 corners next to it, then 6, then
4, then the one opposite. Her whole body resonates with it, and every path
meets again at the far corner, which pops forward and flashes.

**Echo.** Tap two places quickly. Two waves run through her at once. Where they
meet they add up, and those corners swing further and brighter.

**Bloom.** Swipe across her. Swipes add up: each one keeps the spin the last one
left. The faster she turns, the more she opens. Her outer cube flowers out, and
the inner and outer cubes twist against each other. As the spin fades she
closes again.

**Twang.** Press the glass just beside her, not on her, and pull. The corner
nearest your finger stretches out after it like taffy. The rest of her follows,
less the farther each corner is along her edges. Let go and she snaps back,
ringing.

**Slingshot.** Pull her far and let go. She flies the other way, hits the rim
of the glass, flattens on the side that hit, bounces and drifts home. Tap her
while she flies to catch her.

**Shatter and gather.** Pull her all the way to the edge of the glass and let
go: she hits the rim so hard that she bursts into dots all over the screen.
Six quick taps burst her too. No two bursts are alike: her dots fly out along
their own curves, at their own moments, into a few clusters, strands and sprays
with some strays flung right out to the edges. They hang there a moment,
trembling. Sweep your finger through them. Dots near your finger lean towards
it, and every dot you pass curves home with a tick. When the last one is home
she is glad. Leave them alone and they fly home by themselves.

## Tilting her

Tess feels how the device is held, compared with how it usually stands.

**Roll.** Tilt the device and she rolls downhill, gathering speed, bumps into
the edge of the glass, flattens against it and wobbles. Level it again and she
rolls back to the middle, sloshing a little. She is soft, too: tilted, all her
corners sag downhill, the outer cube more.

**Pour.** Tilt the device while her dots hang after a burst. They slide
downhill, each with its own grip, so some stream away and some stick. They
knock against the edges and pile up there. Any dot that slides into her body
stays home. Tilt back and forth and you can pour her back together. While
the device is held tilted, the burst waits for you.

**Knock.** Knock on the device and she rattles: a few corners jiggle and her
dots click, more and louder the harder the knock.

## Her temper

How she answers depends on what has been happening. Play excites her, being
poked too fast annoys her, hard knocks frighten her, and gentle play and petting
make her fond. Each fades on its own, and they colour her moods.

- The same tap rarely gets the same answer. Tap one corner again at once and she
  giggles: a quick, high ring. Excited, she may answer from the opposite corner a
  moment later. Once in a while the wave reflects back.
- Poke her again and again and she gets cross: she pulls the corner in, flinches
  away and grumbles. Wait, or pet her, and it passes.
- Frightened, she shies away from a pulling finger instead of reaching for it.
  Fond, all of her follows it eagerly.
- Cross, she won't bloom as far.
- After play she soon asks for more by herself, with small turns and quiet waves.

## Her sounds

Her sounds come from what her body does, in the same soft, spacey palette as
her voice: no bells and no tunes. A pluck makes her whole body resonate at one
pitch; the corner only changes the overtone and the slow beating over it, so
plucking corner after corner never plays a melody. A pull has a soft, steady
resonance that follows the moving shape and fades as it settles. She
keeps resonating as she spins, thuds softly against the rim (lower for a bigger
Tess), and her dots puff as they knock and come home, a little higher from higher
up the glass. Her mood sets where her voice rests: lower when cross, sad or
sleepy, higher and trembling when scared; a small Tess has a small voice. Her
body sounds stay quieter than her words. Her body sounds never play over
the microphone or a spoken reply, and never while dimmed, in the dark or asleep.

That resonance follows the whole movement, including inertia and the return
after you let go. Speed changes its level; the changing shape colours its layered
overtones. Pitch stays steady within a gesture, and each new gesture has a
slightly different material sound. It fades as she stops. Resting drift, a
stationary tilted pose and automatic recentering stay quiet; spontaneous play
is much softer than your gestures. Holding a pet still accompanies her purr.
Opening a menu releases the sound; Skies and offline particles keep their own music.
The continuous layer also pauses while the agent processes your request.

## When nobody plays

Left alone for a while, Tess plays by herself as her temper
suggests. She plucks herself and a ring runs through her, with a barely audible
soft resonance. One-sided stretching only follows your finger. Excited, she gives
herself a spin and blooms a little. Long alone, two of
her opposite corners wrestle apart and snap back. Each of these is something your
finger can do too; their gentle tension and release have quiet, synchronized sounds.
A touch stops her own play at once. Cross or frightened, she
keeps to herself, and she never starts idle play while dimmed, sleepy or dozing.
Dimming ends current idle play and lets its shape settle without sound.

## Growing

Six discoveries count once each, the first time you find them: Pluck, Echo,
Bloom, Twang, Slingshot (the bounce off the rim), and gathering every dot, by
sweeping or pouring.
A new find makes her flash gold and sing out. Tess starts as a single point. One or
two finds make her a square, three to five a cube, and all six the full
tesseract. Even as a point, a pluck or a spin briefly flashes the full shape
she is growing into. Repeats add nothing, and progress never decays. It
survives restarts, and only a full factory reset clears it. Finds saved by
earlier versions keep their places in the same six steps.

## Skies

To enter directly from Tess’s home screen, hold two fingers near diagonally
opposite corners of the glass for 1.5 seconds. Either diagonal works, even upright
and without an invitation. Keep both fingers in their corner areas; if interrupted,
release both before trying again. This gesture also works after enabling offline play
with BOOT. Menus, setup, agent replies and sleep keep their usual priority.

Lay the cube nearly flat. Now and then one of Tess’s own corners warms gently,
with a quiet low pad that fades in and out; there is no separate floating light;
standing up, she never asks. To ask her yourself, rock the flat cube three times,
quickly, side to side. That only summons her. Ignore it and nothing happens. Touch
the warmed corner, or lift the cube upright while it is visible, and Tess unravels into a
stream of her own dots; musical stars float in the dark. The first sky is Triangulum. One star is already full and emits the stream.
Hold a finger on the glass and move it to guide the light into another star.

Every touch creates an invisible temporary black hole at that point. The stream
bends and swirls around it, gaining a little speed. All black holes, including
permanent ones, are completely invisible; their only visual effect is the motion
of the particles. One or two fingers work the same way
anywhere on the glass; touching a star, source or fixed black hole never selects,
moves or turns it. Each field grows over 0.10 seconds and fades over 0.25 seconds
after release; the other finger keeps controlling its own field. Later skies may
have up to two permanent black holes that shape the stream. Stars keep their
measured relative positions. Tilting is another way to guide the
flow: it pours the stream under full glass gravity in a pouring sky and has a
weaker effect in other skies.

Any active-stream dot can fill any star, whatever their colours. Successful
streams keep feeding their own completed star. Passing through a star gives
the dot that star’s colour. An empty star is a faint radial blur with a little RGB separation, like an unfocused telescope image.
As the stream fills it, the blur narrows and a clear, bright core emerges.
If the stream leaves an unfinished star, it dims and loses focus again.
A completed star keeps its light and becomes the new emitter. The successful
stream from the previous emitter keeps flowing; each discovery adds a lasting stream. There are no orbiting dots, progress rings or segments.
A filling star sparkles softly
in the music; once full, it holds a layered low or middle voice. Passing light also
adds occasional low rhythmic accents, even before a star is full: its octave or
twelfth gives the small speaker a warm body, and the star briefly glows with the beat.
No flow means no accents; dense flow never creates a barrage of notes.
Every full star adds a voice, and black holes hum softly while light swirls nearby.
When every star is full together for a moment, the sky sings its own melody: the
stars in the order you filled them, the slower ones held longer, each flashing as
the chart’s real links appear, then the closing chord. Its English name fades in
only after success; your filling order changes the melody, not the constellation’s figure. Once the melody ends and
the next sky is ready, the old sky fades through darkness over 0.65 seconds; the
new sky begins with a fresh stream from its source. Rarely, a free Aurora sky
arrives with a colourful stream, two permanent black holes, one full emitter and no goals to fill.
After 40 seconds it resolves into the next sky; the game stays open.

Discover eight bright-star figures, from simple to complex:
Triangulum, Crux, Corvus, Delphinus, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, Ursa Minor and Orion.
They grow from three to seven stars. The whole chart may turn, preserving its
proportions; individual stars never drift out of the figure. The catalog repeats
after Orion with new orientations and harmony. Each layout has a verified route
using tilt and one finger, and requires input. There is no score, HUD or Skies
button; the name appears only on completion, and nothing can be lost.
Quiet play, ordinary lifts and tilts keep the game open.
A strong shake or a hardware button ends it; there is no six-sky visit limit.
Her progress is remembered and her motif starts from your last chord. Skies is Tess only and follows the same agent-first rules as the
rest of play below. A short BOOT ends it.

Leaving Skies releases the active game’s voices in about 60 milliseconds and
cancels its delayed notes; the invitation and the agent’s sounds keep their own
playback rules.

## The agent comes first

Play never gets in the way. Pressing KEY, saying Hi Tessa, starting voice
capture or Live, receiving text, speech or a notification, going offline,
opening Setup or a menu, or the screen going dark stops all of it at once. Her
dots spring home and nothing resumes on its own. A touch that arrives together
with such an event is discarded: it cannot dismiss the reply or stop the new
recording. The release of a finger that was pulling or sweeping never becomes
an ordinary tap. During agent thinking or speech the cloud uses the complete
tesseract; recording keeps its sphere.

Screen Lab has a preview of each behaviour for development. Previews never
change saved progress. See [the Screen Lab guide](screen-lab.md).

## Playing without a connection

When Tess has fallen into offline points, press **BOOT briefly** to bring her back
for local play. Touch games and Skies work without Wi-Fi or an agent. The network
indicator stays visible and reconnection continues in the background. In Skies,
BOOT ends the current game; press it again to return to the offline points.
KEY cannot record while disconnected. Reconnection restores normal agent use;
your pairing and preferences stay unchanged.
